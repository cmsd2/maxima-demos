Orbits Around a Black Hole¶

Outside a non-rotating black hole of mass $M$, spacetime follows the Schwarzschild metric. This notebook builds that metric with Maxima's ctensor package and derives the geodesic equations from it. They reduce to a single orbit equation for $u = 1/r$ that differs from Newton's by one term, $3Mu^2$. The effective potential then gives the innermost stable circular orbit at $r = 6M$ and the photon sphere at $r = 3M$.

The numeric half integrates bound orbits with CVODE and measures how far the perihelion advances on each turn. The measured shift matches an exact elliptic-integral formula to many digits. It approaches the weak-field result $6\pi M/(a(1 - e^2))$ far from the hole and exceeds it close in. A last section bends light past the hole and compares the deflection with $4M/b$. Units are geometric throughout, $G = c = 1$, so lengths are in units of $M$.

The Schwarzschild Metric [S]¶

In coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$ the line element is

$$ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{1 - 2M/r} + r^2\,d\theta^2 + r^2\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2$$

ctensor takes the metric as the matrix lg. christof then computes the Christoffel symbols of the second kind into mcs[i,j,k] $= \Gamma^k_{ij}$. Of the nonzero ones, five enter equatorial orbits ($\theta = \pi/2$). The sixth, $\Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta$, vanishes on the equator, so an orbit that starts in the equatorial plane stays there.

Geodesic Equations [S]¶

cgeodesic builds $\ddot x^k + \Gamma^k_{ij}\dot x^i \dot x^j = 0$ for each coordinate, with derivatives taken along the proper time $s$. Setting $\theta = \pi/2$ and $\dot\theta = \ddot\theta = 0$ leaves one equation for each of the other coordinates, and the $\theta$ equation reduces to $0 = 0$. The helper tidy divides each equation by the coefficient of its second derivative and factors each term.

Conserved Energy and Angular Momentum [S]¶

The metric does not depend on $t$ or $\varphi$, so the $t$ and $\varphi$ equations are total derivatives. The conserved quantities are the energy per unit mass $E = (1 - 2M/r)\,\dot t$ and the angular momentum per unit mass $L = r^2\dot\varphi$. The cell checks that $dE/ds$ and $dL/ds$ are multiples of the geodesic equations, so both vanish along any geodesic.

In [4]:
Ecap : (1 - 2*M/r)*'diff(t, s)$
Lcap : r^2*'diff(phi, s)$
[ratsimp(diff(Ecap, s) - (1 - 2*M/r)*geq[1]), ratsimp(diff(Lcap, s) - r^2*geq[4])];
Out[4]:

A massive particle's four-velocity has unit length, $g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu \dot x^\nu = -1$. Writing $\dot t$ and $\dot\varphi$ in terms of $E$ and $L$ turns that condition into the radial equation, a first integral of the $r$ geodesic equation.

The Orbit Equation [S]¶

The shape of the orbit needs $r$ as a function of $\varphi$. With $u = 1/r$ and $u' = du/d\varphi$, the chain rule gives $\dot r = (dr/d\varphi)\,\dot\varphi = -L u'$. The radial equation becomes $L^2 u'^2 = F(u)$, a cubic in $u$. Differentiating with respect to $\varphi$ and dividing by $2L^2 u'$ gives $u'' = F'(u)/(2L^2)$.

The same steps on Newton's energy equation $\tfrac12\dot r^2 - M/r + L^2/(2r^2) = \epsilon$ give the Newtonian orbit equation for comparison.

In [6]:
ueq : expand(subst([r = 1/u, rdot = -L*up], radial))$
F : rhs(ueq)$
orbit_gr : upp + u = expand(diff(F, u)/(2*L^2) + u)$
newton : (1/2)*rdot^2 - M/r + L^2/(2*r^2) = eps$
Fn : L^2*rhs(solve(subst([r = 1/u, rdot = -L*up], newton), up^2)[1])$
orbit_n : upp + u = expand(diff(Fn, u)/(2*L^2) + u)$
transpose(matrix([ueq, orbit_gr, orbit_n]));
Out[6]:

The two equations differ by $3Mu^2$. Relative to the Newtonian term $M/L^2$ it has size $3u^2L^2 \approx 3(v/c)^2$ for a near-circular orbit, which is about $10^{-7}$ for Mercury and of order one near the hole.

The Effective Potential [S+N]¶

Dividing the radial equation by two puts it in the form $\tfrac12\dot r^2 + V(r) = \tfrac12(E^2 - 1)$, with

$$V_{\text{GR}}(r) = -\frac{M}{r} + \frac{L^2}{2r^2} - \frac{ML^2}{r^3}, \qquad V_{\text{N}}(r) = -\frac{M}{r} + \frac{L^2}{2r^2}$$

The extra $-ML^2/r^3$ term overwhelms the centrifugal barrier at small $r$. The Newtonian potential rises to infinity as $r \to 0$, while the relativistic one has a finite peak and then falls into the hole. The plot uses $M = 1$; dashed curves are Newtonian.

Circular orbits sit where $V'(r) = 0$. For $L^2 > 12M^2$ there are two: a stable one at the minimum and an unstable one at the peak. They merge when $V' = V'' = 0$ together, which fixes the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO). The energy there gives the binding energy $1 - E$, the fraction of rest mass that matter radiates as it spirals down to the ISCO through an accretion disc.

A photon has no rest mass, so the $1$ in $(1 + L^2/r^2)$ drops out and its potential is proportional to $(1 - 2M/r)/r^2$. Its single maximum is the photon sphere, where light can circle the hole on an unstable orbit.

A Precessing Orbit [S+N]¶

A bound orbit oscillates between perihelion $r_p$ and aphelion $r_a$, where $u' = 0$. These are two roots $u_1 = 1/r_a$ and $u_2 = 1/r_p$ of the cubic $F(u)/L^2 = 2M(u - u_1)(u - u_2)(u - u_3)$. Matching coefficients gives the third root and $L^2$, so choosing $r_p$ and $r_a$ fixes the orbit.

Perihelion Shift: Exact and Weak-Field [S]¶

Between two perihelia $\varphi$ advances by $2\int_{u_1}^{u_2} du / \sqrt{2M(u - u_1)(u_2 - u)(u_3 - u)}$. The substitution $u = u_1 + (u_2 - u_1)\sin^2\chi$ turns this into a complete elliptic integral of the first kind:

$$\Delta\varphi_{\text{orbit}} = \frac{4K(m)}{\sqrt{2M(u_3 - u_1)}}, \qquad m = \frac{u_2 - u_1}{u_3 - u_1}$$

Expanding $K(m) = \int_0^{\pi/2}(1 - m\sin^2 x)^{-1/2}dx$ in powers of $m$, inserting $u_3 = 1/(2M) - u_1 - u_2$ and expanding in $M$ gives the shift beyond $2\pi$. With $u_1 + u_2 = 2/p$ the first-order term is $6\pi M/p$, the weak-field formula. The second-order term is the next correction.

Perihelion Shift: Numeric Measurement [N]¶

shift_numeric integrates three and a half turns and averages the gaps between successive perihelia, less $2\pi$. shift_exact evaluates the elliptic-integral formula with elliptic_kc. The orbits have $e = 0.5$ ($r_a = 3r_p$) and shrink from $r_p = 1000M$ to $r_p = 8M$. The numeric and exact shifts agree to better than $10^{-8}$ rad in every case. The weak-field ratio starts near one and grows as the orbit approaches the hole, where higher powers of $M/p$ matter.

Light Bending [S+N]¶

A photon follows the same orbit equation with $M/L^2 \to 0$, since $L \to \infty$ while the impact parameter $b = L/E$ stays finite. Taking the limit of the relativistic equation:

In [17]:

A photon arriving from $x = +\infty$ at height $b$ starts at $u = 0$ with $u' = 1/b$ and leaves when $u$ returns to zero. The deflection is the excess of the swept angle over $\pi$. An independent check evaluates the integral $2\int_0^{u_0} du/\sqrt{1/b^2 - u^2 + 2Mu^3} - \pi$ with quad_qags, where $u_0$ is the turning point. The weak-field value is $4M/b$, and the series continues with $15\pi M^2/(4b^2)$.
