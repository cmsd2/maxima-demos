Quantum Wells and the Schrödinger Equation¶

This notebook finds the bound states of a particle in four one-dimensional potentials: the infinite square well, the harmonic oscillator, the finite square well and the double well. One numerical method covers all four. Replacing the second derivative with a finite difference turns the Schrödinger equation into a matrix eigenvalue problem, and np_eig returns the energies and wavefunctions together.

The first three potentials have exact solutions, which we derive symbolically and use to check the matrix method, including its second-order convergence as the grid is refined. The double well has no closed form; there the matrix method shows the tunnelling splitting between the two lowest states and how it shrinks exponentially as the barrier grows. We use units with $\hbar = m = 1$ throughout.

The Schrödinger Equation on a Grid [S]¶

A stationary state $\psi(x)$ with energy $E$ satisfies the time-independent Schrödinger equation

$$-\frac{1}{2}\,\psi''(x) + V(x)\,\psi(x) = E\,\psi(x).$$

On a grid $x_i = x_0 + i h$ the second-difference stencil approximates $\psi''$. Substituting the Taylor series of $u(x \pm h)$ into the stencil shows the leading error is proportional to $h^2$, so halving the spacing should cut the error in each energy by a factor of four.

In [2]:
shifted(s) := sum(diff(u(x), x, j)*(s*h)^j/j!, j, 0, 5)$
stencil : expand((shifted(1) - 2*u(x) + shifted(-1))/h^2);
Out[2]:

Applying the stencil at every interior point, with $\psi = 0$ at both ends of the grid, gives the matrix equation $H\boldsymbol\psi = E\boldsymbol\psi$. The kinetic term contributes $1/h^2$ on the diagonal and $-1/(2h^2)$ on the two neighbouring diagonals; the potential adds $V_i = V(x_i)$ to the diagonal. For four interior points:

In [3]:
H4 : genmatrix(lambda([i, j],
       if i = j then 1/h^2 + V[i] elseif abs(i - j) = 1 then -1/(2*h^2) else 0), 4, 4);
Out[3]:

The numeric version builds the same tridiagonal matrix as an ndarray. np_eig calls LAPACK's general eigensolver, which returns the eigenvalues in no particular order and each eigenvector as a column of unit length. eig_lowest sorts the eigenvalues with np_argsort and returns the lowest m energies with their eigenvectors. The check below runs it on a 200-point well and prints the first few raw eigenvalues, which come out far from sorted, and the norm of one column.

Each eigenvector $\mathbf v$ has $\sum_i v_i^2 = 1$, while a normalised wavefunction has $\int \psi^2\,dx \approx h\sum_i \psi_i^2 = 1$. So $\psi_i = v_i/\sqrt h$. The overall sign of an eigenvector is arbitrary. For plots fix_sign makes each wavefunction rise from the left edge; for comparison with an exact solution align_sign flips a vector whose overlap with the exact one is negative.

Infinite Square Well [S+N]¶

Inside a box $0 < x < L$ the potential is zero and the walls force $\psi(0) = \psi(L) = 0$. With $E = k^2/2$ the equation becomes $\psi'' = -k^2\psi$. ode2 gives the general solution and the left wall removes the cosine.

In [5]:
assume(k > 0, L > 0)$
gen_sol : ode2('diff(u, x, 2) = -k^2*u, u, x)$
left_bc : solve(subst(x = 0, rhs(gen_sol)) = 0, %k2)$
u_box : subst(left_bc, rhs(gen_sol))$
[gen_sol, u = u_box];
Out[5]:

The right wall needs $\sin kL = 0$, so $k = n\pi/L$ and $E_n = n^2\pi^2/(2L^2)$. Normalising fixes the amplitude at $\sqrt{2/L}$. The last line confirms that $\psi_n$ satisfies the Schrödinger equation with energy $E_n$ for every integer $n$.

psi_box(n, x) := sqrt(2/L)*sin(n*%pi*x/L)$
E_box(n) := n^2*%pi^2/(2*L^2)$

For $L = 1$ the matrix on 200 interior points reproduces the lowest five levels. The relative error grows with $n$ because higher states oscillate faster and the grid resolves them less well; the stencil's $h^2\psi''''/12$ term predicts a relative error of $-(n\pi h)^2/12$.

Harmonic Oscillator [S+N]¶

For $V = x^2/2$ the exact states are $\psi_n(x) = H_n(x)\,e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2^n n!\sqrt\pi}$, with $H_n$ the Hermite polynomials from orthopoly, and $E_n = n + 1/2$. Substituting the first six into the Schrödinger equation leaves zero residual, and each integrates to one.

Finite Square Well [S+N]¶

Now $V = -V_0$ for $|x| < a$ and $V = 0$ outside. A bound state has $-V_0 < E < 0$, oscillates inside with $k = \sqrt{2(E + V_0)}$ and decays outside as $e^{-\kappa |x|}$ with $\kappa = \sqrt{-2E}$. The potential is symmetric, so the states are even ($\cos kx$ inside) or odd ($\sin kx$ inside). Matching $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = a$ and dividing out the amplitude gives each condition:

In [13]:
assume(kap > 0, a > 0)$
match(inner) := block([eqs, sB],
  eqs : [subst(x = a, inner) = B*exp(-kap*a),
         subst(x = a, diff(inner, x)) = -kap*B*exp(-kap*a)],
  sB : solve(eqs[1], B),
  ratsimp(subst(sB, eqs[2])/subst(x = a, inner)))$
[even_condition : match(A*cos(k*x)), odd_condition : match(A*sin(k*x))];
Out[13]:

So $k\tan ka = \kappa$ for even states and $-k\cot ka = \kappa$ for odd ones. With $z = ka$ and $z_0 = a\sqrt{2V_0}$ we have $\kappa a = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}$, and the conditions become transcendental equations in $z$ alone. Multiplying through by $\cos z$ or $\sin z$ removes the poles of $\tan$ and $\cot$, so find_root can bracket each root in one quarter-period $[j\pi/2, (j+1)\pi/2]$: even $j$ gives even states, odd $j$ odd ones. For $V_0 = 15$ and $a = 1$, $z_0 = 5.48$ allows four bound states.

Double Well [N]¶

The quartic double well $V(x) = V_0(x^2 - 1)^2$ has minima at $x = \pm 1$ separated by a barrier of height $V_0$. A particle confined to one well would have a ground state near $\omega/2$, with $\omega = \sqrt{8V_0}$ the curvature frequency at a minimum. Tunnelling through the barrier couples the two wells and splits this level into a symmetric ground state and an antisymmetric first excited state. For $V_0 = 10$:

Raising the barrier makes the splitting fall exponentially. The instanton (semiclassical) calculation predicts

$$\Delta E \approx 2\omega\sqrt{\frac{6S_0}{\pi}}\;e^{-S_0}, \qquad S_0 = \int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{2V(x)}\,dx = \frac{4}{3}\sqrt{2V_0},$$

where $S_0$ is the tunnelling action under the barrier. The formula is the leading term of an expansion in $1/S_0$, so its ratio to the exact splitting should approach 1 as the barrier grows, with a shortfall of order $1/S_0$. First the action integral:

The ratio climbs towards 1, and $(1 - \text{ratio})\,S_0$ stays between 1.0 and 1.3 and drifts slowly down, as an expansion in $1/S_0$ predicts.
