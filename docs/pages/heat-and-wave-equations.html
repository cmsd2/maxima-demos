Heat and Wave Equations by the Method of Lines¶

The heat equation $u_t = \alpha u_{xx}$ and the wave equation $u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}$ on $[0, 1]$ with fixed ends both have exact Fourier series solutions. This notebook derives those series symbolically, then solves the same problems numerically by the method of lines: replace $u_{xx}$ with a finite difference on a grid of $N$ interior points, and hand the resulting system of ODEs in time to CVODE.

The two equations lead to very different ODE systems. The heat equation gives a stiff system: implicit BDF reaches $t = 1$ with about 1300 evaluations of the right-hand side, while explicit Euler needs 20000 steps. The wave equation gives an oscillatory system with purely imaginary eigenvalues, where the non-stiff Adams method is the better choice and conservation of energy checks the result.

Separation of Variables [S]¶

Substituting $u = X(x)\,T(t)$ into $u_t = \alpha u_{xx}$ and dividing by $\alpha X T$ gives $T'/(\alpha T) = X''/X$. The left side depends only on $t$ and the right only on $x$, so both equal a constant, written $-k^2$. That gives two ODEs, which ode2 solves.

In [2]:
assume(k > 0, alpha > 0)$
sol_X : ode2('diff(X, x, 2) + k^2*X = 0, X, x);
sol_T : ode2('diff(T, t) = -alpha*k^2*T, T, t);

X = %k1*sin(k*x)+%k2*cos(k*x)
Out[2]:

The boundary condition $X(0) = 0$ removes the cosine, and $X(1) = 0$ then requires $\sin k = 0$, so $k = n\pi$ for $n = 1, 2, \dots$ Each mode $\sin(n\pi x)\,e^{-\alpha n^2\pi^2 t}$ satisfies the equation exactly, and decays at a rate that grows as $n^2$.

Fourier Coefficients of a Triangle [S]¶

The initial temperature is a triangle with peak 1 at the centre, $f(x) = 1 - |2x - 1|$. The sine coefficients are $b_n = 2\int_0^1 f(x)\sin(n\pi x)\,dx$, which integrate evaluates on each half of the interval.

The Discrete Laplacian [S]¶

On a grid $x_i = ih$ with $h = 1/(N+1)$ and $u_0 = u_{N+1} = 0$, the central difference $u_{xx} \approx (u_{i-1} - 2u_i + u_{i+1})/h^2$ turns the heat equation into $d\mathbf{u}/dt = \alpha A\mathbf{u}$, with $A$ tridiagonal. The grid samples $\sin(n\pi x_i)$ of the continuous modes are still eigenvectors of $A$. Applying the difference stencil to them and adding $\frac{4}{h^2}\sin^2(n\pi h/2)$ times the vector leaves zero, which identifies the discrete eigenvalue. Its Taylor series in $h$ shows that it matches the continuous $-n^2\pi^2$ to second order.

Eigenvalues and Stiffness [N]¶

With $N = 99$ ($h = 0.01$) and $\alpha = 1$, np_eig computes the 99 eigenvalues of $A$, which we compare with the formula. The slowest mode decays at nearly $\pi^2$, while the fastest decays at nearly $4/h^2 = 40000$. The ratio of the two is the stiffness ratio. An explicit method must resolve the fastest rate for stability, even though that mode has died out after a tiny fraction of the time of interest.

Solving with BDF [N]¶

CVODE's BDF method handles stiff systems. The right-hand side is the matrix product $\alpha A\mathbf{u}$, passed as a function that also counts how often CVODE calls it. Two references check the result at each output time:

  • the Fourier series of the PDE, summed to 99 terms, which measures the total error;
  • the exact solution of the ODE system, $\sum_n \hat c_n e^{\lambda_n t}\mathbf{v}_n$ with the discrete eigenvalues, which isolates the error of the time integration.

Both references use the matrix $S_{ni} = \sin(n\pi x_i)$.

The Cost of Stiffness [N]¶

The same problem with CVODE's Adams method, which is explicit in character and suits non-stiff problems, needs several times as many right-hand-side evaluations. The counts include the evaluations CVODE spends building finite-difference Jacobians.

Explicit Euler, $\mathbf{u}_{k+1} = \mathbf{u}_k + \Delta t\,\alpha A\mathbf{u}_k$, makes the point more plainly. It is stable only while $|1 + \Delta t\,\lambda| \le 1$ for every eigenvalue, which for the fastest mode means $\Delta t \le 2/|\lambda_{\max}|$, just above $h^2/(2\alpha) = 5\times 10^{-5}$. Reaching $t = 1$ at that step takes 20000 steps. With a step 10% larger, the fastest mode grows by a factor of about 1.2 per step, starting from rounding error.

The Plucked String [S]¶

A string of length 1 is pulled aside to height $a$ at $x = p$ and released from rest. The initial shape is two straight segments. The same separation of variables gives modes $\sin(n\pi x)\cos(n\pi c t)$, and integrate gives the coefficients for a general pluck point.

In [13]:
assume(0 < p, p < 1)$
b_wave : 2*(integrate(a*x/p*sin(n*%pi*x), x, 0, p)
          + integrate(a*(1 - x)/(1 - p)*sin(n*%pi*x), x, p, 1))$
b_wave : factor(ratsimp(b_wave));
Out[13]:

Modes with a node at the pluck point, where $\sin(n\pi p) = 0$, are absent. Each standing wave splits into two travelling waves, which is d'Alembert's form of the solution: $u = \frac12[F(x - ct) + F(x + ct)]$, where $F$ is the initial shape extended to an odd function of period 2. With gradef declaring the derivatives of an arbitrary $F$, Maxima confirms that this form solves the wave equation and starts at rest.

The String as a First-Order System [N]¶

With velocity $\mathbf{v} = \dot{\mathbf{u}}$ the semi-discrete wave equation becomes the first-order system

$$\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}\\ \mathbf{v}\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}0 & I\\ c^2 A & 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}\\ \mathbf{v}\end{pmatrix}$$

of 198 equations. Its eigenvalues are $\pm i\omega_n$ with $\omega_n = (2c/h)\sin(n\pi h/2)$, all on the imaginary axis, so nothing decays and Adams is the natural method. The string is plucked at $p = 0.3$ with $a = 1$ and $c = 1$, so one period takes $t = 2$.

d'Alembert's formula gives the exact PDE solution, and the discrete modes give the exact solution of the ODE system, $\mathbf{u}(t) = \sum_n \hat c_n\cos(\omega_n t)\,\mathbf{v}_n$.

Energy Conservation [S+N]¶

The energy of the string, $E = \frac12\int_0^1 (u_t^2 + c^2 u_x^2)\,dx$, stays constant. At $t = 0$ only the potential term contributes, and the slopes $a/p$ and $-a/(1-p)$ give $E = c^2a^2/(2p(1-p))$. The semi-discrete system conserves the matching sum

$$E_h = \frac h2\sum_i v_i^2 + \frac{c^2}{2h}\sum_{i=0}^{N}(u_{i+1} - u_i)^2$$

exactly, and its initial value equals the continuous one because the pluck point sits on the grid. Any drift in $E_h$ measures the time integrator. BDF, run on the same problem, damps the oscillation slightly, because its higher orders are not stable all the way along the imaginary axis.
